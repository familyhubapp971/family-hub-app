## What this is built on

Four separate research passes: market sizing, the competitive landscape, a
product-market-fit review of what has actually shipped, and a go-to-market
plan. Every number carries its source and date. Where a figure is a
calculation from two sources rather than something anyone published, it says
so. Where no evidence exists, it says that too, rather than guessing.

**These documents disagree with the current strategy in three places.** That is
deliberate, and each disagreement is argued rather than asserted:

1. **Launch market.** [`documents/strategy/saas-transformation.md`](../strategy/saas-transformation.md)
   puts the Gulf first. The research points to the UK first. See
   [where-we-fit.md](where-we-fit.md#the-launch-market-question).
2. **The lead claim.** [`documents/strategy/positioning.md`](../strategy/positioning.md)
   leads with large, blended and multicultural households. Part of that claim is
   not backed by anything that shipped. See
   [where-we-fit.md](where-we-fit.md#what-we-can-honestly-claim-today).
3. **The organisation tier.** The strategy treats schools and clubs as a
   revenue line. The evidence says they are a distribution channel and pay
   almost nothing directly. See [where-we-fit.md](where-we-fit.md#the-second-buyer).

## The one-line version

Family Hub should be sold as **a kids' money and habits app that needs no bank
card**, to parents of children roughly 6 to 11 who already run some kind of
pocket-money arrangement by hand, in the UK first. Everything else it does
(calendar, meals, tasks, learning games) is included, not the reason to buy.

## What is still unknown

The single biggest unknown is whether the invest mechanic works on a real
child. Nobody has built it, so nobody has data, including us. That is either
the moat or the mistake, and 90 days of pilot will tell us which. It is set out
in [product-market-fit.md](product-market-fit.md#the-one-bet-that-decides-everything).
