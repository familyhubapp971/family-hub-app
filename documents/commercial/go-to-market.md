# Go to market: what to say, where to say it, first 100 families

**Written:** 2026-08-08
**Built for:** one person, almost no budget, a working app and no paying users

## What to say

Three messages. Lead with one, keep the others for specific moments.

| Buyer                                     | The line                                                                 | The proof                                                                                                                                                                | The objection it must beat                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Money-lessons parent** (lead with this) | "Pocket money that teaches saving, not just spending. No card, no bank." | A child earns stickers from habits, then spends, saves or invests them, and watches the value grow while the habit holds. Weeks close into a record you can look back on | "Is this just a sticker chart?" Answer: it is a full earn, save and invest loop in a currency you set the value of, before any real card is involved |
| **Overwhelmed parent**                    | "Your family's whole week, in one place."                                | One shared space: today, meals, calendar, tasks, noticeboard                                                                                                             | "I already use Cozi or Google Calendar, free." Answer: those do nothing for the children's side. This does both                                      |
| **Many-children household**               | "Every child gets their own world. You get one screen for all of them."  | A code each on one shared tablet, all their balances on one page. No per-adult charge, no seat limit                                                                     | "Is setting up three children a hassle?" Answer: one device, one code each, five minutes each                                                        |

**Lead with the money line.** It is the only claim nothing else on the market
can make, it is the only one that justifies a subscription rather than "another
calendar app", and financial literacy for children is something parents already
search for and creators already post about.

Keep the other two because they do different jobs. Coordination is why someone
opens the app on a Tuesday, so it belongs in onboarding and inside the product,
not in the first advert. Many-children is a **targeting lever, not a promise**:
most families have one or two children, but larger households get more value and
talk about it more, so it sharpens ad audiences and produces the best
testimonials.

## The money conversation, in the actual words

The product tracks value but never touches real money. Say that plainly, at the
top, before anyone has to wonder. This reassures parents and it keeps us clearly
outside the rules that govern anyone holding or moving funds.

> **We never touch your money.**
> Stickers are not real currency and they never leave your family. You decide
> what a sticker is worth in your own money, and you pay your child yourself,
> the way you always have. Family Hub only keeps score: what each child earned,
> saved and grew. No card is issued. No money moves through us.

> **Is this a bank account?**
> No. Family Hub is not a bank, a wallet, or a place that holds money. We do not
> hold or move a single pound, dirham or dollar.

> **Does my child get a card?**
> No. There is nothing to top up, and nothing for anyone to lose or misuse.

> **What does "invest" actually do?**
> It is a lesson, not a market. Your family sets the rule: stickers grow a
> little on each day the habit is kept, and can shrink a little on a day it is
> missed. Your child feels what steady effort does to a balance, using stickers,
> long before real money is involved.

> **Who holds the money?**
> You do. Family Hub is a private record your family controls, worth exactly
> what you say it is worth.

That last line does the important work without using a legal word. It makes
clear the stickers cannot be spent outside the family and are not a payment
instrument. **Before any launch that implies real money, have a lawyer confirm
this framing** against the UK's e-money rules and, if the Gulf comes later, the
UAE Central Bank's stored value facility rules. Nothing in this document is
legal advice.

**Two words to use carefully.** "Invest" and "grows" are the right words for a
UK audience. If the Gulf follows, revisit them: a balance that increases over
time sits close to a concept that carries real weight in a Muslim-majority
market, even though ours increases through effort rather than time, which is
materially different. This is a copy decision, not a reason to skip the market.

## Closing the trust gap

There is no app store, so there is no store review, no badge, and no implicit
"someone checked this". A parent is being asked to put their children's
information into a website. That trust has to be built on the page.

**What has to be on the page, in this order:**

1. A plain line high up: "Each family gets its own private space. Nobody outside
   your family can see it."
2. Who sees what: parents see everything, children see only their own world,
   and we do not browse family data.
3. **A named founder with a face and one line of why.** For a product this
   personal, a real person beats a faceless company.
4. Real screenshots of the actual product. Not stock photos of smiling families.
5. A direct answer to "is my child's information safe": where the data lives,
   that it is encrypted, and that nothing is sold or shown to advertisers.
6. Testimonials with a first name and a town. Five real families beats "loved
   by thousands".

**How to get the first proof:** run a founding-families group from the personal
network, 20 to 30 people, in exchange for early access. Ask each for two lines
and permission to use their first name and town. That is the first page of
social proof and it costs nothing but asking.

**App store, later not now.** Add-to-home-screen (a PWA) is the right call
today: it installs like an app, costs nothing, and waits on no review. A real
store listing is worth building once a few hundred families are paying and
people start asking "is there an app", because by then the badge earns its
maintenance and the store's cut.

## Channels, cheapest first

| #   | Channel                                                                       | Money          | Hours                          | First customer                   | The two-week test, under $200                                                             |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Referral inside the product                                                   | $0             | Low, once built                | Needs 20 to 50 families first    | Build the invite link now so it is ready                                                  |
| 2   | Parenting groups on Facebook and WhatsApp (Mumsnet, NCT, local school groups) | $0             | 5 to 8 a week                  | 1 to 3 weeks                     | Join 5, post something useful 2 to 3 times a week, tag the links so clicks are countable  |
| 3   | Small creators, 5k to 30k followers, parenting and money                      | $0 to 150 each | 4 to 6 to find and message 20  | 2 to 4 weeks                     | Offer 5 creators free lifetime access plus a small fee, one tracking link each            |
| 4   | Schools, nurseries and clubs                                                  | $0             | 6 to 10 a week, mostly chasing | 4 to 8 weeks, they are slow      | Email 15 offering a free 10-minute slot at a parents' evening                             |
| 5   | Writing for real searches ("pocket money app", "chore chart for siblings")    | $0 to 50/mo    | 3 to 4 per article             | 8 to 12 weeks, then it compounds | Publish 2 articles on exact long-tail phrases, submit to Search Console                   |
| 6   | Small paid ads on Meta or TikTok                                              | up to $200     | 2 to 3                         | 1 to 2 weeks for data            | $200 split by message, measure cost per **signup**, not per click                         |
| 7   | Search ads                                                                    | up to $200     | 1 to 2                         | 1 to 2 weeks                     | Expect a high cost per signup: nobody is searching for a category that does not exist yet |
| 8   | Press                                                                         | $0             | High, unpredictable            | Unpredictable                    | Not a two-week test. Treat any coverage as trust, not volume                              |

Start at 1 and 2 in week one because they are free and start the flywheel. Layer
in 3 and 4 across weeks two to six. Only spend the $200 once the page converts
and signups can actually be measured.

## The first 100 families

| Week | What to do                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Finish the trust and money copy above. Build the invite link. Track two things only: signups, and "first sticker earned". Join 5 parenting groups as a real member |
| 2    | Message 30 to 50 people in the personal network. Offer founding-family access. Target 15 to 20 signups. Ask the first 5 for a testimonial                          |
| 3    | Publish 2 articles. Post 3 useful, non-salesy things in the groups                                                                                                 |
| 4    | Message 20 small creators. Give 5 of them free lifetime access and a small fee                                                                                     |
| 5    | Email 15 schools, nurseries and clubs offering a free demo slot. Expect slow replies, follow up weekly                                                             |
| 6    | Start the $200 advert test, one advert per message. Let it run two weeks                                                                                           |
| 7    | Email the families you have (40 to 60 by now) with a direct referral ask. Publish the first testimonial                                                            |
| 8    | Creator posts go live. Track signups per link. Message 20 more creators                                                                                            |
| 9    | School replies land. Run the demo or send a flyer home. Target 20 to 30 signups                                                                                    |
| 10   | Publish 2 more articles. Post on Indie Hackers for credibility, not volume                                                                                         |
| 11   | Read the advert results, kill the weaker platform. Second referral push                                                                                            |
| 12   | Count every channel by cost per **activated** family. Put the last push into the best two                                                                          |

Rough path to 100: personal network 15 to 20, groups and writing 10 to 15,
creators 15 to 25, schools and clubs 30 to 40, referral 10 to 15, adverts 5 to 10. Ranges, not promises.

## What to measure

| Stage          | The number                                                                               |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Interest       | Page visits, and what share sign up                                                      |
| **Activation** | Share of new families where a **child** signs in and earns a first sticker within 3 days |
| Use            | Families active this week, children signing in, stickers earned                          |
| Staying        | Week 4 family retention                                                                  |
| Money          | Free to paid conversion, and cost per family by channel                                  |
| Spread         | Invites sent per activated family, and how many convert                                  |

**The one number to watch before there is any revenue: activation.** Revenue
lags weeks behind a channel's real quality. Activation shows within days, and it
tells you whether the people a channel sends actually try the thing that makes
Family Hub different. A channel with cheap signups and low activation is buying
curiosity, not families.

**The second, as soon as there are any activated families:** invites sent per
family. If families who like it are not inviting anyone, the cheapest long-term
channel is not going to work, whatever is spent elsewhere.

## What this plan assumes, and could be wrong about

- That the personal network contains 30 to 50 reachable parents of 6 to 11 year
  olds. If it does not, week 2 fails and everything shifts right.
- That parenting groups tolerate a founder participating. Many ban promotion
  outright, so the "be useful first" rule is not optional politeness.
- That schools reply at all in August. Term timing matters more than the week
  numbers above suggest: aim the school push at the start of a term.
- That $200 buys enough data to judge a channel. It probably buys a direction,
  not a conclusion.
