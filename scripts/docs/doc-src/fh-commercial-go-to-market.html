Go to market

What to say, where to say it, and how to get the first hundred families. Built for one person with almost no budget.

The plan in one line Lead with the money message, not the calendar. Build trust on the page, because there is no app store to borrow it from. Start with the two free channels, referral and parenting groups, and only spend the first $200 once signups can actually be measured. Watch activation, not signups.

01 · MessageWhat to say

Buyer The line The objection it must beat
Money-lessons parent
(lead with this)		 "Pocket money that teaches saving, not just spending. No card, no bank." "Is this just a sticker chart?" It is a full earn, save and invest loop in a currency you set the value of, before any real card is involved
Overwhelmed parent "Your family's whole week, in one place." "I already use Cozi, free." Those do nothing for the children's side. This does both
Many-children household "Every child gets their own world. You get one screen for all of them." "Is setting up three children a hassle?" One device, one code each, five minutes each

Lead with the money line. It is the only claim nothing else can make, the only one that justifies a subscription, and financial literacy for children is something parents already search for. Coordination is why someone opens the app on a Tuesday, so it belongs in onboarding, not the first advert. Many-children is a targeting lever, not a promise: most families have one or two children, but larger households get more value and talk about it more.

02 · TrustThe money conversation, in the actual words

The product tracks value but never touches real money. Say that plainly, at the top, before anyone has to wonder.

We never touch your money Stickers are not real currency and they never leave your family. You decide what a sticker is worth in your own money, and you pay your child yourself, the way you always have. Family Hub only keeps score: what each child earned, saved and grew. No card is issued. No money moves through us.
The question The answer
Is this a bank account? No. Family Hub is not a bank, a wallet, or a place that holds money. We do not hold or move a single pound, dirham or dollar.
Does my child get a card? No. There is nothing to top up, and nothing for anyone to lose or misuse.
What does "invest" actually do? It is a lesson, not a market. Your family sets the rule: stickers grow a little on each day the habit is kept, and can shrink a little on a day it is missed.
Who holds the money? You do. Family Hub is a private record your family controls, worth exactly what you say it is worth.

That last line does the important work without using a legal word. Before any launch that implies real money, have a lawyer confirm this framing. Nothing here is legal advice.

03 · CredibilityClosing the web-app trust gap

There is no app store, so no store review, no badge, and no implicit "someone checked this". A parent is being asked to put their children's information into a website. That trust has to be built on the page.

  • A plain line high up: "Each family gets its own private space. Nobody outside your family can see it."
  • Who sees what: parents see everything, children see only their own world, and we do not browse family data.
  • A named founder with a face and one line of why. For a product this personal, a real person beats a faceless company.
  • Real screenshots of the actual product. Not stock photos of smiling families.
  • A direct answer to "is my child's information safe": where the data lives, that it is encrypted, that nothing is sold.
  • Testimonials with a first name and a town. Five real families beats "loved by thousands".

App store, later not now. Add-to-home-screen costs nothing and waits on no review. A real store listing earns its keep once a few hundred families are paying and people start asking "is there an app".

05 · ExecutionThe first hundred families

Week What to do

2 Message 30 to 50 people in the personal network. Target 15 to 20 signups. Ask the first 5 for a testimonial
3 Publish 2 articles. Post 3 useful, non-salesy things in the groups
4 Message 20 small creators. Give 5 free lifetime access and a small fee
5 Email 15 schools, nurseries and clubs. Expect slow replies, follow up weekly
6 Start the $200 advert test, one advert per message. Let it run two weeks
7 Email the 40 to 60 families you have with a direct referral ask. Publish the first testimonial
8 Creator posts go live. Track signups per link. Message 20 more
9 School replies land. Run the demo or send a flyer home. Target 20 to 30 signups
10 to 12 Two more articles. Read the advert results and kill the weaker platform. Second referral push. Count every channel by cost per activated family and put the last push into the best two

Rough path to 100: personal network 15 to 20, groups and writing 10 to 15, creators 15 to 25, schools and clubs 30 to 40, referral 10 to 15, adverts 5 to 10. Ranges, not promises.

06 · MeasureThe one number before revenue

Watch this Activation: the share of new families where a child signs in and earns a first sticker within three days. Revenue lags weeks behind a channel's real quality. Activation shows within days, and it tells you whether the people a channel sends actually try the thing that makes Family Hub different. A channel with cheap signups and low activation is buying curiosity, not families.

The second, as soon as there are activated families: invites sent per family. If families who like it are not inviting anyone, the cheapest long-term channel will not work, whatever is spent elsewhere.

07 · CaveatsWhat this plan assumes

  • That the personal network contains 30 to 50 reachable parents of 6 to 11 year olds. If not, week 2 fails and everything shifts right.
  • That parenting groups tolerate a founder taking part. Many ban promotion outright, so "be useful first" is not optional politeness.
  • That schools reply at all in August. Aim the school push at the start of a term.
  • That $200 buys enough data to judge a channel. It probably buys a direction, not a conclusion.
