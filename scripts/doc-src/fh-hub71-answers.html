Q2 What are you building and how is it different than what is already out there?

One private space for the whole family, not one app per problem.

Family Hub gives each household a single space. Calendar, meals, tasks and notices for the grown-ups. A PIN login and a world of their own for every child, from age four, with no email address and no phone needed.

Everything connects, and that is the point.

Habits stick when the reward lands straight away and the progress is visible. That is exactly what the product does. A child ticks a habit off, earns stickers on the spot, and watches the streak build. Teeth brushed, bed made, reading done, bins out. Each one leaves them a little better off than yesterday.

Stickers then convert to real pocket money at a rate the parent sets. The parent can boost that rate for the habit that matters most this month, or invest the child's stickers so they grow week on week. A seven-year-old learns what saving feels like, and what compounding feels like, by taking out the bins.

On their own, that is a job list, some pocket money and a maths lesson. Put together, they become one thing: a child who picks up good habits and learns how money really works, without ever sitting down for a lesson. No debit card is involved, which is why it works from age four, and why it works for parents who would never hand a young child a card.

Parents are not buying a chore app. They are buying a childhood where the good habits are the easy ones.

Screen time that gives something back.

Each child's world has games for maths, logic and flags of the world, and parents can see how they are getting on. So bedtime stops being an argument about how long they were on the screen, and becomes a question about what they learned.

Built for the family you actually have.

Twins, blended homes, a grandparent who needs access, a child too young to own an email address. Optional cultural lenses and flexible calendars for multicultural homes. The product bends to the household, instead of asking the household to tidy itself up first.

Every family stays private, and that gives us a second business.

Every family gets their own private space. Nothing crosses between homes. That same wall lets a school, nursery or club run Family Hub under its own name. So we grow two ways: one family at a time, or a hundred when a school signs up.

The difference is not a longer list of features. It is that one small moment does four things at once: the job gets done, the habit sticks, the child earns, and the money teaches. Repeat that for a few years and you get a child with good habits and a real grip on money. That is what parents are buying, and why they stay.

Q3 What is the 'Why Now' for your business?

The "mental load" is now a mainstream conversation.

Parents have a name for the thing that was wearing them out, and they use it out loud. That is new. It means we no longer have to explain the problem before we sell the answer, which is usually the hardest and most expensive part of building anything for families.

We did not take that on trust. We spoke to 24 parents, watched how they actually run their week, and put the product in front of them. Ten of those families are using it in their own homes today. The same three things came back every time: the week lives in one person's head, chores turn into a fight, and screen time feels like something the family loses rather than gains. We built for those three, in that order.

Screen-time guilt has peaked.

Parents have stopped trying to ban screens and started asking what the screen gives back. A product that turns screen time into habits, savings and learning answers that question head on.

Building this got cheap enough to do properly.

AI changed what a small team can ship. We built a production, multi-tenant platform for a fraction of what it used to cost, which means we can serve ordinary families at a price they will actually pay.

And the UAE just made the family a national priority.

2026 is the UAE's Year of the Family, declared alongside the National Family Growth Agenda 2031 and a new Ministry of Family. Family life is not a side topic here, it is government policy with attention behind it. Abu Dhabi is already built around families: households are larger, several generations live close, and schools, nurseries and clubs sit at the centre of daily life. Those are exactly the doors our second business walks through.

And it is personal.

I am building this as a parent, for the week my own family was losing. I have spent 15 years delivering software people rely on in retail, media, travel and finance, and I am doing it here now, on ADCB's Open Finance programme, so I know this market and how things get built in it. The problem is mine, the market is on my doorstep, and the country has just said out loud that this is the decade to solve it.

Q5 What success metrics do you measure, and where are you with each of them?

Real families using it, not people saying they like it.

We are pre-revenue, so these are the honest numbers. We interviewed 24 parents. Ten of those families now run Family Hub at home, on their own phones, through the whole journey: signup, kid PIN login, the parent dashboard, the reward economy and the learning modules. Their feedback comes back to us in-app and by survey, and that is what we steer the build with.

The five numbers we watch, and what each one tells us.

  • Families onboarded. Signup done, a child added, a habit set. Ten today. This is the headline number.
  • Families active in a week. The only honest read on whether we earn a place in their week.
  • Habits completed per child per week. Tells us the loop is working, not just installed.
  • Weeks closed by a parent. Proof the money loop was used, not just looked at.
  • Week-4 retention. Families still there a month after onboarding.

The one number we steer by: kept weeks.

Learning apps lose people faster than any other kind of app. The usual answer is points and streaks that mean nothing the moment you close the app.

Ours pays real money, and there is a parent on the other side of it. A child cannot keep a streak on their own, and a parent cannot fake one.

So we count kept weeks: a week where the child did their habits and the parent paid out. Four in a row and we have become part of how that family runs. That is the number every change we make has to move.

What switches on at launch.

Paid tiers go live during the programme. From that point we measure three more things: free to paid conversion, month-3 retention, and revenue per family. We would rather report those numbers than predict them.

Q13 Who is responsible for building your product?

We build it ourselves. Nobody outsources their own home.

Babatunde owns the product and builds it, directing AI engineering agents under a strict process: written acceptance criteria for every feature, a review pass, and automated tests that must go green before anything reaches a family.

This is not a prototype held together with tape. It is a production platform: every family's data sealed off from every other, sign-in and payments running on proven infrastructure, and a full staging environment so nothing untested touches a real home.

The business point is simple. There is no agency to brief, no queue to wait in, and no invoice between an idea and a shipped feature. When a family tells us something is wrong, we can fix it that week, and almost every dollar invested goes into reaching families rather than into salaries.
